    Step 2 · Hear real radio amateurs

    Open receivers, live

    There are receivers connected to big antennas that anyone can control from their browser. Nothing to install, no licence needed. Try the 40-metre band in the late afternoon or evening: you'll hear people chatting from half of Europe.

    On the air now

    When one radio amateur hears another, they often post it online: it's called a spot, a heads-up. Together they make a living map of who is talking to whom all over the world, right now. Each line is a signal that has crossed countries or oceans.

      Learn to recognise them

      What's that sound?

      When you first listen to the radio, it all sounds like beeps and noise. Each sound has its own shape on the waterfall, the coloured graph on receivers. Tap one: you'll hear it and see how it's drawn.

      Choose a sound to hear and see it
      0 Hz1,0002,0003,000 Hz

      Time falls downwards and pitch goes from low (left) to high (right). Just like on a real receiver.

      The sounds are generated in your browser, imitating the real ones. Afterwards, look for them on a real receiver with the button that appears here.

      And this is how they really sound

      The sounds above are clean imitations. On the air there's noise, fading and voices talking over each other. These are real, freely licensed recordings: listen and compare. While they play, they are drawn on the waterfall above.
